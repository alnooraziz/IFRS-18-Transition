# Transitioning from IAS 1 to IFRS 18 — Briefing Note

Nov 22, 2026 · @Alnoor · For colleagues and clients

## At a glance

**IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements*** (issued by the IASB on 9 April 2024) replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning **on or after 1 January 2027**, including interim reports in that year. It applies **retrospectively** with restated comparatives; early application is permitted and must be disclosed. Recognition and measurement do not change — this is a presentation and disclosure standard, but it changes what investors see on the face of the income statement.

## What changes

|Area|IAS 1 today|IFRS 18|
|-|-|-|
|Income statement structure|Few required subtotals; "operating profit" undefined and inconsistent|Five categories: **operating, investing, financing, income taxes, discontinued operations**; two required subtotals: **operating profit** and **profit before financing and income taxes**|
|Management-defined performance measures (MPMs)|Outside the financial statements, unaudited|Subtotals used in public communications (e.g., adjusted EBIT) disclosed in **one note**, reconciled to the nearest IFRS subtotal, with tax and NCI effects per item — **within audit scope**|
|Aggregation and disaggregation|General materiality guidance|Explicit principles; operating expenses by nature, function or mixed; if by function, disclose depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment and inventory write-downs in a note; "other" only when no better label exists|
|Cash flows (IAS 7 amendments)|Indirect method could start from various profit lines; free choice on interest and dividends|Indirect method starts from **operating profit**; classification choices for interest and dividends largely removed|

Entities whose **main business activity** is investing or providing finance (banks, insurers, investment entities, some holding companies) classify certain investing and financing income and expenses in the operating category — this assessment is a key early judgement.

## Why it matters now

For a December year-end, **FY2026 is already the comparative year** and the first IFRS 18 interim (Q1 2027) must also restate its comparative. Expect knock-on effects on KPIs, debt covenants, remuneration metrics tied to EBIT/EBITDA, ERP mappings and investor messaging. In the first year, entities must reconcile each income statement line item as restated under IFRS 18 to the amounts previously reported under IAS 1. Confirm local adoption status with your regulator (e.g., SECP notification in Pakistan) before fixing dates.

## Recommended transition plan

1. **Gap assessment** — map current income statement lines, notes and cash flow policies to IFRS 18 requirements.
2. **Classification judgements** — assess main business activities; allocate every income and expense line to a category; document the rationale.
3. **MPM inventory** — sweep annual reports, investor decks, press releases and analyst calls for non-IFRS subtotals; decide which to keep and build reconciliations.
4. **Systems and data** — update the chart of accounts, consolidation tool and reporting templates to capture category tags and nature-of-expense data.
5. **Dry run** — prepare restated FY2026 comparatives and Q1 2027 interim mock-ups; agree them with auditors early.
6. **Stakeholder impact** — review covenant definitions, bonus metrics and guidance; brief the board, audit committee, lenders and investors.
7. **Governance and controls** — put controls over MPM data in place, since it becomes audited.

**Bottom line:** profit does not change, but how it is presented and explained does. Start with classification and MPMs — they drive most of the effort and the most visible change for users.
