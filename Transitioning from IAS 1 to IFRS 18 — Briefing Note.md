# Transitioning from IAS 1 to IFRS 18 — Briefing Note

Nov 23, 2026 (rev. 2) · @Alnoor · For colleagues and clients

## At a glance

**IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements*** (issued by the IASB on 9 April 2024) replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning **on or after 1 January 2027**. It applies **retrospectively**, including to comparative information and to interim reports in the first year. Early application is permitted and must be disclosed. Recognition and measurement do not change: this is a presentation and disclosure standard, but it changes what investors see on the face of the income statement. Several IAS 1 requirements are retained in IFRS 18 or relocated, mainly to IAS 8, renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*.

## What changes

| Area | IAS 1 today | IFRS 18 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income statement structure | Few required subtotals; "operating profit" undefined and inconsistent | Five categories: **operating, investing, financing, income taxes, discontinued operations**. Operating is the **residual** category. Two new required subtotals: **operating profit** and **profit before financing and income taxes** (with exceptions for some entities with specified main business activities) |
| Management-defined performance measures (MPMs) | Outside the financial statements, unaudited | Subtotals of income and expenses used in public communications to convey management's view of an aspect of entity-wide performance (e.g., adjusted EBIT). A rebuttable presumption applies that such subtotals do represent management's view. Disclose in **one note**: explain each measure, reconcile it to the **most directly comparable** IFRS-defined subtotal or total, and give tax and NCI effects per reconciling item. **Within audit scope**. Not every KPI or ratio is an MPM; listed subtotals such as gross profit and operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and IAS 36 impairments are excluded |
| Aggregation and disaggregation | General materiality guidance | Explicit principles and informative labelling; "other" only when no better label exists. Operating expenses by nature, function or a mix, whichever gives the most useful structured summary. If by function, disclose in a single note: depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, IAS 36 impairment losses and inventory write-downs |
| Cash flows (IAS 7 amendments) | Indirect method could start from various profit lines; free choice on interest and dividends | Indirect method starts from **operating profit**. Default: interest and dividends **received → investing**; interest and dividends **paid → financing**. Entities with specified main business activities follow their income statement classification |

## Key judgement: banks, NBFCs, leasing companies and insurers

Entities whose **main business activity** is investing in assets or providing financing to customers classify certain income and expenses in the operating category that would otherwise be investing or financing. The assessment is based on facts and circumstances and made **for the reporting entity as a whole**, so consolidated and separate financial statements can differ.

For a qualifying finance or lease business:

- Interest income on customer loans and finance leases, and related credit impairment, are **operating**.
- Interest on borrowings that **relate to providing financing to customers** is **operating** (required, not a choice).
- For other pure-financing liabilities there is an **accounting policy choice** between operating and financing. If the entity cannot distinguish the two, it classifies income and expenses from all such liabilities as operating.

**Watch:** the IFRS Interpretations Committee is still discussing how these rules apply within groups (June and November 2026 meetings). Document the policy, monitor the outcome and agree significant judgements with auditors early.

## Why it matters now

For a December year-end, **FY2026 is already the comparative year**, and the first IFRS 18 interim (Q1 2027) must restate its comparative too. In the first annual IFRS 18 financial statements, reconcile each income statement line item as restated to the amount previously reported under IAS 1; specific reconciliations also apply in first-year interim reports. Reclassification alone does not change reported profit.

Review consequential amendments, including **IAS 7, IAS 8, IAS 33** (additional EPS figures only on permitted numerators) and **IAS 34** (MPM disclosures in interims). Expect knock-on effects on KPIs, debt covenants, remuneration metrics tied to EBIT/EBITDA, ERP mappings, investor messaging and regulatory reporting.

**Pakistan:** confirm SECP's adoption notification and dates. Then check how IFRS 18 interacts with the formats prescribed under the Companies Act 2017 schedules and, for banks, by SBP before fixing implementation dates.

## Recommended transition plan

1. **Gap and impact assessment.** Map current income statement lines, notes and cash flow policies to IFRS 18; quantify reclassifications and the revised subtotals.
2. **Classification judgements.** Assess main business activities; allocate every income and expense line to a category; document the policy choices, the rationale and the auditors' conclusions.
3. **MPM inventory.** Sweep annual reports, investor decks, press releases and analyst calls for non-IFRS subtotals; identify which qualify as MPMs, decide which to keep, and build the reconciliations.
4. **Systems and data.** Update the chart of accounts, consolidation tool and reporting templates to capture category tags and nature-of-expense data; validate comparative and parallel reporting.
5. **Dry run.** Prepare restated FY2026 comparatives and Q1 2027 interim mock-ups; test the required reconciliations and agree them with auditors.
6. **Stakeholder impact.** Review covenant definitions, bonus metrics and guidance; brief the board, audit committee, lenders and investors.
7. **Governance and controls.** Assign owners and deadlines; put controls in place over categories, disaggregation, MPM data and transition disclosures, since they become audited.

**Bottom line:** profit does not change, but how it is classified, subtotalled and explained can change a lot, especially for finance entities. Prioritise main-business-activity judgements, MPMs, comparative reconciliations and early auditor engagement, and confirm the local regulatory position before locking in dates.
